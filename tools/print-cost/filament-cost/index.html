How to calculate filament cost

Divide spool purchase price by net filament mass, excluding the weight of the empty spool. Multiply that cost per gram by total slicer consumption. For a spool sold as 1 kg, use 1,000 g unless the actual usable mass is different. PLA, PETG and other materials use the same mass-based arithmetic.

The formula behind the estimate

Material = used ÷ package size × package price
Power = hours × watts ÷ 1,000 × price/kWh
Machine = hours × machine rate
Repeat reserve = (material + power + machine) × p ÷ (1 − p)
Whole plate: divide plate costs equally by item count
Add successful labor, extras, allocated setup and retry labor
Price/item = (cost/item + fixed fee ÷ quantity) ÷ (1 − margin − selling fee rate)

A worked example · per item, no selling fees

A 1,000 g spool costing 24 gives a cost of 0.024 per gram. A 75 g job uses 1.80 of material. A second job using 150 g uses 3.60. Change the other cost fields to 0 if you only want the filament component, and set margin to 0 to keep the output at cost.

Failure probability p, margin and selling fee rate are decimals in the formula. Setup is once per order. Retry labor is the extra effort per failed attempt, multiplied by p ÷ (1 − p). Keep input totals consistent when switching between per-item and whole-plate mode.

Read the pricing method or compare assumptions with Prusa’s calculator. This estimate does not know market demand or your local tax rules.

Notes from the workshop

Should I enter the model weight or total filament?

Enter total filament consumed for the job, including supports, purge and any unusable material allocated to it.

Can I estimate multiple filament colors?

This version has one material rate. Use a weighted average cost per gram or calculate each material component separately and add the costs.

Does the currency selector convert prices?

No. It changes how numbers are displayed. All entered costs must already be in the currency you select.
