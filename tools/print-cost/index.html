The method, without the guesswork

What does a 3D print really cost?

Start with the material weight from your slicer, including supports and purge waste. Use the purchase price and usable mass of your spool to get the material cost. Then add average power use, machine wear, hands-on work and any packaging or consumables.

The formula behind the estimate

Material = used ÷ package size × package price
Power = hours × watts ÷ 1,000 × price/kWh
Machine = hours × machine rate
Repeated costs = (material + power + machine) ÷ (1 − failure rate)
Total cost = repeated costs + labor + extras
Price = total cost ÷ (1 − target margin)

A worked example

For 75 g from a 1,000 g spool costing 24 currency units, material costs 1.80. Five hours at 120 W and 0.18 per kWh adds 0.108. With 0.50 per machine hour, a 5% failure rate, 20 minutes of labor at 18/hour and 1 of extras, cost is about 11.64. At 30% margin, the price is about 16.63.

Percentages in the formula are expressed as decimals. Use your own inputs and compare the result against completed jobs. For more context, read the 3D printing pricing guide and Prusa’s printing cost calculator.

At the workbench

Where do I find material weight and print time?

Use the estimates from your slicer. Include support and purge material. Replace estimated print time with observed time when you have it.

Is the example a recommended market price?

No. The example inputs demonstrate the formula. Enter your actual purchases, measured power consumption and business costs.

Does quantity account for a shared build plate?

No. Quantity multiplies the per-item estimate. Divide shared machine time and hands-on labor by item count before entering them if your items share one batch.
