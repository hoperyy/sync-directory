SHOW YOUR WORK

How to estimate resin printing cost

Use the total resin volume from your slicer, including supports. Divide the bottle price by its volume in mL, then multiply by mL used. Include your hands-on washing, support removal and curing work in labor, and allocate gloves, solvent and other consumables to extras. Machine and power inputs should cover the equipment costs you choose to include.

The formula behind the estimate

Material = used ÷ package size × package price
Power = hours × watts ÷ 1,000 × price/kWh
Machine = hours × machine rate
Repeat reserve = (material + power + machine) × p ÷ (1 − p)
Whole plate: divide plate costs equally by item count
Add successful labor, extras, allocated setup and retry labor
Price/item = (cost/item + fixed fee ÷ quantity) ÷ (1 − margin − selling fee rate)

A worked example · per item, no selling fees

A 1,000 mL bottle costing 30 gives 0.03 per mL. A 40 mL print consumes 1.20 of resin. A 1 kg bottle is not automatically 1,000 mL: divide its 1,000 g mass by the density in g/mL supplied by the manufacturer.

Failure probability p, margin and selling fee rate are decimals in the formula. Setup is once per order. Retry labor is the extra effort per failed attempt, multiplied by p ÷ (1 − p). Keep input totals consistent when switching between per-item and whole-plate mode.

Read the pricing method or compare assumptions with Prusa’s calculator. This estimate does not know market demand or your local tax rules.

Notes from the workshop

Can I enter resin weight in grams?

The form is volume-based. Convert grams to mL using the resin’s density, and use volume for both the consumed amount and the bottle size.

Are washing and curing included automatically?

No. Add their hands-on time, consumables and any equipment expense that you want to allocate. Default values are examples.

How do I price several models on one plate?

Whole plate mode divides entered plate totals across identical items. It does not calculate how many models fit or automatically predict slicer time. For different models, use separate estimates or a deliberate cost allocation.
